MUDPUPPY

Brand book · Salvage Yard

Tag it, strip it, ship it.

MudPuppy walks a legacy portal like a scrapyard: it logs every part it can see, tags the ones worth keeping, and lets people draw the new build over them. The brand is steel plate, spray-paint orange, manila tags and drafting lines: gritty enough to have character, serious enough for an enterprise architecture review.

01

What we are

Mission

MudPuppy documents what an application actually does today, before anyone migrates it. It observes the running app, records evidence, and turns that evidence into business-analysis documentation. Every statement points back to what was seen.

For whom

Legacy developers, BAs and QAs on enterprise portal migrations, who spend all day in tools like Sitecore or AEM and use an AI harness to do the walking. People who read evidence for hours.

The metaphor

A salvage yard. The old portal is the lot; screens are parts; records are tags; the to-be is a blueprint drawn over the parts; people's notes are typed on the tags. Nothing is thrown away until someone decides.

02

Personality

Four traits. If a choice fights one of them, the choice is wrong.

Honest

Shows what was observed, marks what was guessed, never fills a gap with a guess.

Workmanlike

A tool on a workbench, not a showroom. Dense, square, labelled.

Dry humour

One yard joke per screen at most, and never in an error or a number.

Easy on tired eyes

Dark steel, warm chalk text, no glow over data, no motion you didn't ask for.

09

Misuse

Don'tDraw the wordmark as an image, outline it, or set it in any font but Archivo Expanded.
Don'tRecolour the mark, add glow or gradients, or place it on a light background.
Don'tUse marker orange for success, red for "warning", or blueprint for anything that is real.
Don'tRound corners, add drop shadows, or lay grain, rust or grime textures over data.
Don'tSet machine output in the typewriter face. It is reserved for people.
Don'tPut two primary buttons, or two intent plates, in one view.
